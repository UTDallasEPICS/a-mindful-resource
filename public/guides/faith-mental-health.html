A Guide for Believers, Families & Faith Leaders

Faith and Healing Were Never Meant to Be Enemies.

Honoring the strength of our faith while making room for mental health care — because both can be true. Seeking help is not a lack of faith. Sometimes it is the bravest act of it.

In crisis? Call or text 988 | Text HOME to 741741
01

Both things can be true

Our faith has carried this community through every storm — and prayer is real power. The church has been our refuge, our family, our place to exhale.

And a mental health struggle is not a punishment, a demon you failed to pray away, or proof your faith is weak. Depression, anxiety, and trauma are real conditions — the same way diabetes or high blood pressure are real. You can hold tight to your faith and reach for care at the same time. One does not cancel the other.

02

Untangling what we were taught

Gently, with love:

  • “If you had more faith, you’d be fine.” Faithful people still get sick — in body and in mind. Needing help is human, not faithless.
  • “Just pray it away.” Prayer and treatment aren’t rivals. Many believers find their healing through both.
  • “We don’t air our business.” Silence has cost too many of us too much. Telling a trusted helper isn’t gossip — it’s care.
  • “Therapy is for other people.” Therapy is for anyone carrying something heavy — and that’s all of us, at some point.
03

Your faith is real strength

Hold on to this — we’re not asking you to trade any of it. We’re asking you to add care to it:

  • Community care: A community that prays for you and checks on you.
  • Sustaining hope: Hope and meaning when the road is dark.
  • Grounding practices: A rhythm of rest, worship, and gratitude that steadies the soul.
  • Belonging: A refuge where you are known and loved.
04

Faith and care, together

Both / and:

  • God works through people: Doctors, counselors, and medicine can be part of the answer to a prayer.
  • Tending your mind is stewardship: The mind God gave you is worth caring for, like the body and the spirit.
  • You can find faith-friendly care: It’s okay to ask a therapist if they’ll respect and include your faith. Many will.
  • Healing in community is biblical: “Carry each other’s burdens.” You were never meant to carry yours alone.
05

A word to the shepherds: for faith leaders & churches

Pastors and ministry leaders are often the first call when a family is hurting. You don’t have to be a clinician to be a bridge to healing.

  • Name it from the pulpit: When leaders speak openly about mental health, shame loses its grip on the pews.
  • Know when to refer: Pair prayer with a warm hand-off to professional care when someone is in crisis.
  • Build a mental health ministry: A trained team, a list of trusted providers, a safe space to talk.
  • Partner with us: AMRI equips faith leaders with tools to support their congregations — trust first, prevention before crisis.
06

Keep the faith — and get the help.

They were never opposites. Lean on your God, your people, and the care that is there for you. Healing is not a betrayal of your belief. It can be the answer to it.
