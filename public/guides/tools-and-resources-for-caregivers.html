A Guide for the Ones Who Hold Everyone Else

You Take Care of Everyone. Who Takes Care of You?

Tools, support, and permission to care for yourself — while you care for someone you love. You can’t pour from an empty cup. Filling yours is part of the work.

01

We see the unseen work

Maybe you’re caring for an aging parent, a sick spouse, a grandchild you’re raising, or a loved one living with mental illness, addiction, or a disability.

In our community, this is love and duty — often quiet, often unpaid, and often landing on the women and the grandparents who “just handle it.” It is sacred work. It is also heavy, and it can wear you down in ways no one sees. This guide is about you.

02

Caregiver burnout is real

You can love someone deeply and still be running on empty. Burnout isn’t weakness — it’s your body and mind telling you that you need support too:

  • Exhaustion: Always exhausted, even after you rest.
  • Frustration & guilt: A short fuse, resentment, or guilt for feeling it.
  • Neglecting self: Letting your own health, meals, and checkups slide.
  • Isolation: Pulling away from friends and the things that used to bring you joy.
  • Loneliness: Feeling alone in it, like no one truly sees what you carry.
You Matter Too If you ever feel you can’t go on, reach out — call or text 988, anytime, free and confidential.
03

Carry it differently: tools for the load

  • Share the load: Make a list of tasks and let others pick some up. People want to help — give them a way in.
  • Accept help: “Yes, thank you” is a complete sentence.
  • Build in respite: Even a few hours off keeps you in this for the long haul. Respite isn’t abandonment — it’s maintenance.
  • Get organized: One folder or app for documents, medications, and contacts saves your sanity on the hard days.
  • Keep your checkups: You can’t care for them if you fall apart. Your appointments matter too.
  • Name the grief: Watching someone change or decline is its own quiet loss. Let yourself feel it.
04

Caring for someone with mental illness or addiction

This is its own kind of heavy. Hold on to these truths:

  • The three C’s: You didn’t cause it, you can’t control it, and you can’t cure it.
  • Set limits: You can love someone and still set limits. Boundaries protect the relationship — and you.
  • Support without losing yourself: Their recovery is theirs to walk; your wellbeing is yours to protect.
  • You’re not alone: NAMI’s Family-to-Family course and family support groups are built for exactly this.
