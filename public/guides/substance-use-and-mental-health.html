A Guide for Anyone Carrying Pain — or Loving Someone Who Is

Pain You Don’t Heal, You Often Carry — or Numb.

Understanding the link between substance use and mental health — with compassion, not shame, and real paths to healing. Addiction is not a moral failure. It’s pain looking for relief — and healing is possible.

In crisis? Call or text 988 | Text HOME to 741741
01

Let’s begin without shame

Many people use substances to cope with pain — trauma, loss, anxiety, depression, and the daily weight of stress, survival, and racism. That is not weakness or a lack of willpower. It’s a human response to pain, and it is treatable.

Our community has too often been met with handcuffs instead of help — punished for addiction while others were treated for it. You deserve care, not a cage. This guide leads with that.

02

Mental health & substance use travel together

  • Hand in hand: Anxiety, depression, trauma (PTSD), and substance use frequently occur together — this is called co-occurring or “dual” diagnosis.
  • Self-medication is common: Many people use substances to quiet pain that was never treated. Healing that underlying pain is part of recovery.
  • Treating both matters: The best care addresses the mental health and the substance use together — not one at a time.
Need Immediate Support? SAMHSA’s free, confidential helpline can point you to treatment near you: 1-800-662-HELP (4357), available 24/7. If you or someone you love is in danger, call or text 988.
03

When it’s becoming a problem

For yourself or someone you love — some signs it may be time to seek support:

  • Increased use: Using more, or more often, just to feel okay.
  • Tolerance & withdrawal: Needing more to get the same effect, or feeling sick without it.
  • Loss of control: Trying to cut back and not being able to.
  • Secrecy & shame: Hiding use or carrying heavy shame around it.
  • Impact on life: It’s costing relationships, work, health, or peace.
04

Pathways to healing

Recovery isn’t one road, and it isn’t all-or-nothing. It’s real — and people reach it every day through diverse avenues:

  • Therapy and counseling: To treat the pain underneath, not just the symptom.
  • Support groups: AA, NA, and culturally-rooted recovery communities where you’re understood.
  • Medication-assisted treatment (MAT): For some, medication plus counseling is the most effective path. It’s treatment, not “trading one drug for another.”
  • Harm reduction: Meeting people where they are and keeping them alive and safer. Naloxone (Narcan) reverses opioid overdoses and is available at most pharmacies without a prescription.
  • Treatment programs: Outpatient or residential, matched to what someone needs. FindTreatment.gov locates them near you.
05

Loving without losing yourself

If you love someone struggling with substance use:

  • The three C’s: You didn’t cause it, you can’t control it, you can’t cure it.
  • Support without enabling: You can love someone and still set boundaries that protect you both.
  • Take care of yourself too: Al-Anon and Nar-Anon exist for families and friends. You matter in this.
  • Keep the door open: Shame pushes people deeper; steady, boundaried love invites them back.
  • Carry Naloxone (Narcan): If someone you love uses opioids, keep naloxone on hand — it’s free or low-cost at many pharmacies and health departments, and it reverses overdoses while waiting for help. Always call 911.
06

A word on shame

The thing that keeps people sick is shame. Shame whispers “you’re bad” instead of “you’re hurting” — and it keeps people from reaching out.

Compassion, for yourself or your loved one, is what helps people heal. You are not your worst day, and recovery doesn’t require you to earn your way back to dignity. You already have it.

07

Trusted resources

Get help now, connect with community support, and find culturally-responsive care.

  • SAMHSA National Helpline Free, confidential treatment referrals and information, 24/7, in English and Spanish. 1-800-662-4357
  • FindTreatment.gov Find substance-use and mental-health treatment locations near you.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline For any crisis, free and confidential, 24/7. Call or text 988.
  • Narcotics Anonymous & Alcoholics Anonymous Free peer recovery communities with meetings almost everywhere.
  • Al-Anon & Nar-Anon Support groups dedicated to the families and friends of people who use substances.
  • Therapy for Black Girls & Therapy for Black Men Culturally-responsive therapists focusing on the pain underneath.
  • A Mindful Resource Inc. Trust-first support & connection. info@amindfulresource.org • 214-506-0691

Healing is possible — and you’re worth it.

However long the road has been, it can change. Reach out without shame, for yourself or someone you love. There is help, there is community, and there is a way back to yourself.
