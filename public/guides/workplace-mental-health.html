A Guide for Working Professionals & Teams

You Shouldn’t Have to Leave Your Whole Self at the Door.

Protecting your mental health at work — and knowing the supports and rights you already have. Your job is what you do. It is not the whole of who you are.

In crisis? Call or text 988 | Text HOME to 741741
01

The weight you carry to work

Most of us spend the majority of our waking hours at work. For many Black professionals, that comes with extra weight — being the only one in the room, code-switching all day, absorbing microaggressions, and feeling you have to be twice as good to be seen as equal.

That takes a real toll, even when the paycheck looks fine. Naming it is not complaining — it’s the first step to protecting your peace.

02

Naming the load: what it actually costs

  • Code-switching fatigue: Performing a different version of yourself all day is exhausting. The tiredness is real.
  • Microaggressions add up: The “small” comments and slights accumulate into a heavy weight.
  • Hypervisible & invisible at once: Watched more closely, recognized less often. That tension wears on you.
  • “Imposter” feelings: Often this isn’t a personal flaw — it’s a reasonable response to real bias. Call it what it is.
03

Protect your peace: guarding your mind at work

  • Take breaks and PTO: Rest isn’t a reward you earn — it’s maintenance.
  • Find your people: An affinity group, an ERG, or one trusted colleague can be oxygen.
  • Set boundaries: You don’t have to answer every email at 9pm. Protecting your off-hours protects your mind.
  • Separate worth from work: A hard day at the job is not a verdict on your value.
  • Build in micro-resets: A walk, a breath, a step outside between meetings keeps you steady.
04

What you already have: supports & rights

You likely have more support than you know:

  • Your EAP: Most employers offer an Employee Assistance Program — free, confidential counseling sessions. Many never use it because they don’t know it exists. Ask HR.
  • Accommodations are your right: Under the ADA, mental health conditions can qualify for reasonable accommodations — and you don’t have to share your diagnosis to use general supports.
  • FMLA for serious conditions: You may be entitled to protected leave to care for your health or a loved one’s.
  • Privacy choice: You decide what to disclose. Using support and protecting your privacy are not mutually exclusive.
05

When it’s bigger than a bad day: when work is the problem

Some workplaces are genuinely harmful. Chronic disrespect, discrimination, or a toxic culture is not something you have to absorb forever.

  • Document what’s happening: Dates, details, witnesses — quietly kept records protect you.
  • Health outranks any job: If a workplace is breaking you down, it’s okay to get support and to look for the door.
  • Know your legal rights: The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) explains your rights and how to file a complaint at eeoc.gov. You don’t have to navigate discrimination or harassment alone.
06

Trusted resources

Talk to someone, find a culturally-responsive therapist, or learn about our workplace training.

  • Your Employee Assistance Program (EAP) Free, confidential counseling through your employer. Ask HR for the number — you may already have sessions waiting.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline Free, confidential support 24/7. Call or text 988.
  • Therapy for Black Girls & Therapy for Black Men Culturally-responsive therapists who understand the weight you’re describing.
  • Open Path Collective Affordable therapy sessions when cost is a barrier.

You can show up, do good work, and still guard your mind and your dignity. Take the break, use the support, hold your boundaries. You are more than your output.
