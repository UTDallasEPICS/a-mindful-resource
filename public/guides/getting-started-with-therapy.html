A Guide for First-Timers & the Therapy-Curious

Therapy Isn’t Weakness. It’s Maintenance for Your Mind.

What therapy really is, how to find the right person, and how to make it affordable — demystified. You don’t have to be in crisis to deserve support. You just have to be human.

In crisis? Call or text 988 | Text HOME to 741741
01

Let’s clear the air

A lot of us grew up hearing “what happens in this house stays in this house,” or that therapy is for white folks, or for people who are “crazy.” None of that is true.

Therapy is for anyone carrying something — grief, stress, anxiety, old wounds, or just the weight of being strong for everyone else. Going isn’t weakness. It’s wisdom.

02

What therapy actually is (and isn’t)

  • What it is: A trained, confidential professional who helps you understand yourself, cope, and grow.
  • What it isn’t: Not someone telling you what to do, judging you, or analyzing you on a couch.
  • Not just for crisis: Many people go to maintain their mental health — like a checkup for the mind.
  • Not forever: Some go for a season, some longer. You set the pace.
03

Finding the right therapist

  • Look for culturally-responsive care: A therapist who shares or respects your background — who you don’t have to teach about racism or your community — can change everything.
  • “Interview” them: It’s okay to ask about their experience, approach, and whether they’ve worked with people like you.
  • The first fit isn’t final: If it’s not a fit after a few sessions, it’s okay to switch. That’s not failure — it’s self-respect.
04

Making it affordable

  • Insurance: Many plans cover therapy — call the number on your card and ask about “behavioral health.”
  • Sliding scale & Open Path: Open Path Collective offers sessions around $30–$80 when you don’t have good coverage.
  • Your EAP: If you work, you may have free sessions through an Employee Assistance Program. Ask HR.
  • Community clinics & training programs: Low-cost care exists in most areas.
  • Group therapy: Often more affordable — and healing happens in community.
05

What to expect & how to start

  • Nerves are normal: Most people feel nervous, especially the first time.
  • You set the pace: You never have to share more than you’re ready to.
  • Confidentiality: What you say stays private, with rare exceptions for safety (like if you’re in danger).
  • Start small: A first phone call or a single session is a real, brave beginning.
06

If you’re not ready yet

That’s okay too. You can still take a step: talk to a trusted person, join a support group, lean on your faith community, or call or text 988 if you’re struggling.

And our door at AMRI is open — trust first, no pressure.
