A Guide for Parents, Mentors & Village

Our Kids Are Carrying More Than They Say.

Understanding what today’s young people are facing — and how to show up for them, long before it becomes a crisis. They don’t need you to be perfect. They need you to be present.

In crisis? Call or text 988 | Text HOME to 741741
01

The world they’re growing up in

Today’s young people are navigating pressures earlier generations never had: a phone in every pocket, 24/7 comparison, relentless school stress, a frightening news cycle, and a loneliness that hides behind being “connected.”

For Black and Brown youth, the load is heavier still — carrying the weight of racism and code-switching before they’re grown, being seen as older and “less innocent” than they are, living near community violence, and having far less access to care that looks like them and gets them. They are remarkably resilient. They are also tired. Seeing them clearly is the first act of love.

02

What you might be seeing

Pain doesn’t always look like sadness. Especially in boys, depression and anxiety often show up as anger, numbness, or “I don’t care” — because we teach our sons to hide hurt. Watch for changes, not just tears:

  • Pulling away: Withdrawing from family, friends, and things they used to love.
  • Anger & irritability: For many boys, this is what depression looks like.
  • School changes: Slipping grades, skipping school, or trouble focusing.
  • Sleep & energy: Sleeping too much or too little, big changes in appetite or energy.
  • Risky behaviors: Substance use or a sudden “nothing matters” attitude.
  • Warning signs: Giving away what they value, or talking about being a burden or not wanting to be here.
If They Talk About Not Wanting to Be Here Take it seriously and don’t leave them alone. Ask directly — asking does not plant the idea, it opens a door. Call or text 988, or text STEVE to 741741 for a counselor trained to support young people of color. In immediate danger, call 911.
03

How to show up: trust first

  • Lead with trust: “I’ve noticed you seem off lately — I’m here, no judgment” opens more than “What’s wrong with you?”
  • Listen more than you fix: Often they need to be heard, not solved.
  • Validate before advice: “That sounds really hard” opens the door. “You’ll be fine” closes it.
  • Ask the direct question: “Are you thinking about hurting yourself?” It’s brave — and it can save a life.
  • Normalize getting help: Talk about counseling like you’d talk about a doctor for a broken arm.
  • Keep showing up: Trust is built in small, repeated moments — the car ride, the late-night snack, the game on the porch.
04

The phone in their pocket: the comparison trap

  • The comparison trap: They’re measuring their behind-the-scenes against everyone else’s highlight reel.
  • Set loving limits: Phone-free meals and no screens in the bedroom at night protect sleep and peace.
  • Stay curious: Ask what they love about their apps — you’ll learn a lot, and they’ll feel respected.
  • Watch the late-night spiral: Endless scrolling, cyberbullying, and comparison quietly feed anxiety and depression.
05

Trusted resources

Crisis support, youth organizations, and culturally-responsive mental health networks.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline Free, confidential support 24/7. Call or text 988.
  • Crisis Text Line A trained counselor by text, anytime. Text HOME to 741741.
  • The Steve Fund Crisis support with counselors trained for young people of color. Text STEVE to 741741.
  • The Trevor Project Crisis support for LGBTQ+ young people. Call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678.
  • The Jed Foundation (JED) Mental-health support and resources built for teens and young adults.
  • The AAKOMA Project Mental health for youth of color and their families.
  • NAMI Education, support groups, and family resources nationwide.
  • Therapy for Black Girls & Therapy for Black Men Find a culturally-responsive therapist who gets your child.
  • A Mindful Resource Inc. Our youth programs — Bro Chill™ and Real Talk. Real Skills.™ — build trust and emotional skills before crisis. info@amindfulresource.org • 214-506-0691

You don’t need all the answers. You just have to stay in the room.

Your steady, loving presence helps more than the perfect words ever could. Keep showing up — they’re worth it, and so are you.
